# Product

## Register

brand

## Users
Two audiences share one link. Couples planning a wedding build and send the invitation from the AisleFlow dashboard, often on a laptop, in short evening sessions. Their guests receive a personal link, usually by text or WhatsApp, and open it on a phone, sometimes standing in a queue, sometimes sitting with a partner deciding whether they can come. The guest's job: feel invited, learn when and where, and reply in under a minute.

## Product Purpose
AisleFlow turns a wedding invitation into a personal web page that opens like real stationery, then quietly handles the practical parts (RSVP, meal choices, schedule, photos, gifts). Success is a guest saying "that was beautiful" before they say "that was easy", and a couple feeling their invitation looks as considered as printed stationery from a good studio.

## Brand Personality
Romantic, calm, crafted. The invitation speaks like a hand-written card, never like software: no "portal", no "account", no step counters shouting in capitals. Premium comes from restraint and one or two hand-made touches, not from adding more.

References:
- **Vowpost** (vowpost.com/i/grace-noah): simplicity. One beautiful layout, a CSS envelope that opens in three gentle beats, a full-bleed photo with the names over it, flat sections without boxes.
- **Cardia Studios** (Royal Gold, Wine, Quiet Luxury): premium feel. Script names, engraved illustrations in the accent colour, light pages alternating with rich colour bands, a calendar with a hand-drawn heart, an RSVP that begins "Dear Guest,".
- **Areone** (Classic): the stationery frame and paper feel, used sparingly.

## Anti-references
- Areone's busier themes: many buttons, outlined boxes, three type styles competing on one screen, novelty themes.
- Anything that reads as an app or form builder: sans-serif body text on the invitation, bright system greens and reds, progress bars, cards around every section, a tiny tracked label above every heading.
- Heavy video intros that make phones wait.

## Design Principles
1. **It's stationery first.** Every screen should look like it could be printed. Interface chrome only appears where a guest has to act.
2. **One gesture, one moment.** The opening is a single tap that feels physical, then gets out of the way.
3. **The couple's colours, everywhere.** Every element takes its colour from the theme; nothing is hard-coded to a brand or system colour.
4. **Restraint is the luxury.** One display face, one text face, one accent. Add a hand-made detail before adding another element.
5. **Fast on a phone.** Nothing beautiful is worth a loading bar.

## Accessibility & Inclusion
WCAG 2.2 AA: body text at 4.5:1 or better on its background, tap targets of at least 44px, every animation with a reduced-motion alternative, script fonts only for names and headings (never for body text or form labels), and content that is visible even if an animation never runs.
